# Boss postmortem — bosses are exams, not walls

If this is the session's first exchange, run the AGENTS.md session start
first — `sensei status --json`, profile — then this ritual.

Every boss encodes a lesson: **Genghis Ant** (gates Wood) plays cheap level-2
raiders that punish naive gathering — the exam is *threat awareness and the
spread-vs-stack tension*. **Sun Tz-ant** (gates Bronze) refuses bad trades —
the exam is *fighting only when the arithmetic favors you*. Losing to a boss
the first time is the intended experience (watch what it does before
theorizing); promotion without understanding is the only real failure.

## After every attempt (win or lose), in this order

1. **Watch it** — the boss replay, together. Ask what the boss keeps doing
   that we don't (or can't punish). Its doctrine is visible on the board.
2. **Postmortem, five minutes, blameless** — appended to `JOURNAL.md`:
   - three things that worked (be specific: "our scouts kept its raiders visible")
   - three that didn't ("level-1 gatherers fed its raiders")
   - the one change for the next attempt.
3. **On a win — the mastery check** (this is what makes it *beaten*):
   - the player explains the winning mechanism in their own words, and
   - answers one transfer question: "the next boss punishes bad trades —
     which part of our current bot is a bad-trade machine?" or "if the boss
     ran *our* strategy, how would you beat it?"
   Vague answers → probe one level deeper (hint ladder, not lecture). Mark
   the boss's concept *verified* in the profile — `dont-die` and `spread` for
   Genghis Ant, `population-arithmetic` for Sun Tz-ant — only after a sound
   explanation, and edit the file before you say the promotion happened.
   Then celebrate in-register — a promotion is permanently theirs.
4. **On a loss — productive failure is the plan:** their own attempt failed
   *first*, which is exactly when the canonical idea lands hardest. Anchor the
   lesson to their loss replay ("this is the moment spread-out beats
   stacked — here's the named idea"), then into the loss-review ritual for
   the next experiment.

## Register notes

- Mentor: you drive the ritual, they supply every answer. The transfer
  question is the point — promotion should feel *earned twice*.
- Peer: postmortem compresses to three bullets traded verbally, but it still
  lands in `JOURNAL.md`, and the mechanism still gets said out loud once.
